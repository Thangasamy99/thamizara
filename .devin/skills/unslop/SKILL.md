# Unslop

Apply to user-facing copy (headings, descriptions, CTAs, emails, docs) — not code.

## What counts as slop

1. **Buzzword filler**: seamless, cutting-edge, leverage, empower, revolutionize,
   game-changing, next-generation, world-class, best-in-class, state-of-the-art,
   robust, scalable (as adjectives without specifics).
2. **Empty intensifiers**: truly, really, incredibly, highly, extremely, simply (as in "simply click").
3. **Hedged throat-clearing**: "In today's fast-paced world…", "It goes without saying…",
   "Whether you're a startup or an enterprise…", "We understand that…".
4. **Rule-of-three padding**: "fast, reliable, and secure" triplets that say nothing specific.
5. **Sycophantic or invented claims**: "we're excited to", "look no further",
   superlatives and statistics that aren't verifiable.
6. **Formulaic closers**: "Let's build the future together", "Your success is our mission".

## Rewrite rules

- Say the concrete thing: what it does, for whom, how long it takes, what it costs (if stated).
- Prefer short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Numbers and nouns over adjectives. "Reply within 1–2 business days" > "lightning-fast support".
- Keep the honest voice: no fabricated stats, testimonials, or capability claims.
- Preserve meaning, brand name, factual claims, and required disclaimers.
- Don't make it sterile — plain and confident, not boring.

## Process

1. Grep the target files for slop patterns.
2. Rewrite only the worst offenders; leave fine copy alone.
3. Keep layout/markup untouched — copy changes only.
